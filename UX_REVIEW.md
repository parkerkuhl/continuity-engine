# UX review: Continuity Engine web app

Reviewed `main` at `0250376` (after PR #13), 2026-11-24. Evidence is a seeded world built through the real MCP server (38 interlinked pages, 7 secret sections, 2 chapters, 5 scenes, 15 updates, 1 conflict) and 155 screenshots at 1440px and 390px in light and dark. Rerun them with `node apps/web/e2e/ux-screenshots.mjs` after building `@continuity/mcp` and `@continuity/web`. They land in `ux-screenshots/` (gitignored). File names below refer to that folder.

Parker's answers that shape this report (2026-11-24): secrets stay hidden until the player knows them, unless you're looking as the GM; the player view should not reveal that a secret exists; the web app gets a New page button; no brand or tone rules yet.

## Executive summary

The reading experience is the strongest part of the app. Serif prose at 17px with a comfortable measure, a clean dark mode, clear type grouping, and aliases indexed for search. A practiced user finds a minor character's fact in 4 actions, about 12–15 seconds on desktop and about 20 on a phone. The weak spots are all about trust and spoilers. When the AI edits a page directly, the change skips the review queue, carries no label on the page, and can put a fact the player character doesn't know into the player view. The player view also tells you *that* secrets exist. The review queue buries the one item that needs a decision, a conflict, under every draft page, and its counts disagree from screen to screen. Users also can't create a page by hand. The biggest opportunities are: mark and surface every AI change where it happens, put conflicts and updates first in the queue, remove every hint that a secret exists from the player view, and make search jump straight to the page.

## What's working (keep it)

- **Reading comfort.** Source Serif 4 at 17px / 1.6 line height, about 75 characters per line in the main column, `###` subheads styled as small caps. Dark mode tokens give cards clear separation and readable secondary text (`22-entity-abbess-secret-pc-knows_player_desktop_dark.png`).
- **Always knowing where you are.** The sidebar's World / Campaign box with chapter and as-of moment, the breadcrumb, and "World: The Lantern Coast · Chapter 2" on the dashboard (`10-campaign-settings_player_desktop_light.png`).
- **Aliases are first-class.** "heron" finds Wren Tallow; "Also called" sits under every title (`13-search-alias-heron_player_desktop_light.png`).
- **The conflict card.** "GM proposes" next to "Current canon · your locked section 'Wants'", with Keep / Edit before deciding / Accept and overrule (bottom of `32-review-queue_gm_desktop_light.png`).
- **The edit-confirm question.** Retcon versus "Change from now on" is the right question, worded in plain language (`41-edit-confirm-dialog_desktop_light.png`).
- **Server-side spoiler filtering.** Secret sections, GM notes and unknown updates never reach the player view's HTML, and search in player view ignores matches that exist only in secret text: "admiralty" finds 2 pages in player view and 4 in GM view (`16-…_player`, `35-…_gm`).
- **The "Secret · the PC knows it" state** for a secret the player has learned (`22-…`).
- **Undo is always one click away** in History, and undoing is itself a new version.
- **URL-driven state.** Search, type, source, view and tab are all in the URL, so Back works and links can be shared.
- **Inline errors on forms**, for example "That file isn't a readable .zip." (`50-import-error_desktop_light.png`).

## Bigger recommendations

### A. One place that shows everything the AI changed (findings 3, 4, 9, 17, 22)

**Before:** AI changes reach the user three different ways. Scene updates go to the queue. Direct page edits show only in a page's History tab. Pending proposals against a locked section show on the queue but not on the section. None of them show a diff, and AI text on a page looks like the user's.

**After:** the review queue becomes "What changed", grouped by scene: the scene summary, then its conflicts, updates and page edits, each with a word diff and Keep / Undo / Edit. Every section on a page carries a provenance line ("GM · ch-02, scene 1 · 2 h ago") and a dot until seen. A section with a pending proposal shows it inline. Read models only, except #1.

### B. Search as the main way around (findings 10, 11, 12, 14, 26)

**Before:** type, Enter, results page, click, scroll. No typo tolerance, no hint why something matched, no links between pages.

**After:** `/` from anywhere opens the search box with a quick-jump list (names and aliases, "matched alias: the Heron"). Enter on the highlighted result goes straight to the page. Typos fall back to the fuzzy matcher. Names inside page text link to their pages. Alias URLs redirect. Target for the timed task: 2 actions, under 6 seconds.

### C. Secrets that stay secret until the player learns them (findings 1, 2, 13, 21)

**Before:** the AI's page edits skip "who knows", the player view counts hidden things, and GM view is easy to forget once the switch scrolls away.

**After:** a fact the PC doesn't know can only reach a page as GM-only until `secrets_learned` or the user reveals it (needs your OK, since it touches `edit_entity` and the protocol prompt). The player view gives no sign that anything is hidden. GM view is marked at all times, and making something public asks first.

### D. Creating and onboarding by hand (findings 5, 24, 25)

**Before:** pages come only from Claude or a .zip. The first-run screen shows a database path and unexplained choices.

**After:** a New page button in the wiki and on each type heading, with a short form that opens straight into the page's outline. The first-run screen offers "Build with Claude", "Import" and "Start empty", with one line explaining each approval mode.
