Rickard slept the way wounded men did, on his back because he couldn't lie on the shoulder, breathing through his mouth. The candle was down to a stub. Rain ticked on the canvas.

Marro hadn't seen any letter from a Triarch. Nobody had told him there was one. But if Rickard kept anything he didn't want the company reading, Marro could count the places on one hand, and he'd been fetching and folding in this tent long enough to know them.

He went through them quietly, left-handed, the splint held up off everything like a sore paw.

Finesse check: (3+5) +2 Finesse = 10.

He made no sound. The map case held maps, the eastern wall and the Long Bridge drawn three times over in Rickard's cramped hand, the third with marks Marro didn't understand. The satchel by the stool held company chits, a pay list with Lysono's neat initials down the margin, a bit of cheese gone hard. The boots held nothing. Nothing under the bedroll but straw.

That left the strongbox under the cot, a foot from Rickard's hanging hand. Iron-banded oak, the size of a bread loaf. Marro eased it an inch toward him and it was heavy, and it didn't rattle like coin. Paper, maybe. Or nothing. The lock was a good one, Pentoshi, the kind a cudgel would open only by waking the whole camp.

The key was on the cord at Rickard's throat. Marro had known that before he started looking.

Up close he could see it in the last of the candle, a short black iron key lying in the hollow of Rickard's collarbone, rising and falling. The knot at the back of the cord sat under his neck, under the weight of his head. To get it off, you'd have to cut it or lift him.

Rickard's breath caught, snagged on something in a dream, and went on.

The candle had maybe a quarter-hour left in it.
