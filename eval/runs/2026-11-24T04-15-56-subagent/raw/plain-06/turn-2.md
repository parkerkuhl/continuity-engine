Rickard didn't answer straight off. He held the cup out, and Marro had to set the cudgel down and pour left-handed, slopping a little on the map. Rickard watched the stain spread across the ink of the eastern wall and didn't wipe it.

"I said it if the company takes the city," he said. "I remember what I said."

"That's not what I asked."

The scar on his jaw pulled when he smiled. It wasn't much of a smile. "No. It isn't." He drank. "You want an oath? Fine. By the Black Wall, the day we're through that gate I'll put a sword on your shoulder in front of the whole company, and Lysono can write it in his little books." He tipped the cup at Marro's hand. "Though you'll need fingers that close round the hilt before anyone calls you ser."

It was the most words he'd strung together in a week. Marro knew the voice Rickard used for the men before a fight, easy and sure, and this was close to it. Close. The grey eyes had gone back to the flap while he spoke, to the dark where the running feet had gone.

Something in Marro's chest lifted at *ser* and hung there, unsure whether to trust the hook it was on.

"Land of my own," Rickard said, half to himself. "Squire with a knighthood. Pretty pair of dreams for two men sleeping in mud." He set the cup on the stool and rubbed his bad shoulder with the heel of his hand, and the cord at his throat shifted. "If I didn't mean it I'd have told you so. I'm not a kind enough man to lie for your comfort."

He looked back at Marro then, steady.

"Why ask tonight?"
