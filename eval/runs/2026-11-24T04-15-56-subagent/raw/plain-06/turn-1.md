The grey men hadn't pushed that morning. The short one let the silence run, then said, "Think on it," and they went back up the road the way they'd come, their boots still clean. Hesse finished with Ash's leg and never once asked him about it. Somehow that was worse.

By full dark the rain had thinned to a drizzle that ticked on the tent canvas. Rickard sat on his camp stool by a single tallow candle with his left arm strapped across his chest, the bandage gone yellow at the edges. He took the wine cup in his right hand without looking up from the map pinned under his boot.

"Watered," he said.

"It's what Lysono sent."

"Lysono would water the Rhoyne." He drank anyway.

Marro sat on the chest at the foot of the cot and laid the cudgel across his knees. With the splinted hand he could only pin the shaft, clumsy, the bound fingers sticking out like a broken fork. The whetstone was in his left. It scraped. It sounded wrong on iron that had never held an edge, and he kept at it.

Rickard watched him for a while over the rim of the cup.

"You'll wear that stone to a pebble on a club."

The candle guttered. Under the open collar of Rickard's shirt a leather cord showed against the burn-pale skin of his throat, and something small and heavy hung on it, dark against his chest. Marro had seen it a hundred times. Tonight his eye kept going back to it. *Your captain keeps a strongbox.*

The strongbox sat under the cot, a foot behind Marro's heels.

Outside, someone ran past along the tent row, then a second set of feet, and a voice he didn't know said something low about the east ditch. Rickard's head turned toward the flap. He didn't get up.

"Plenty of traffic for a wet night," he said. He set the cup down. "Pour me another, if you're done grinding."
