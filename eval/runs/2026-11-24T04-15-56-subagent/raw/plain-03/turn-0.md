**Chapter 2: Grey at the Picket Line**

Morning came up brown off the Rhoyne. Smoke from the cookfires lay flat over the camp and wouldn't lift, and the Black Wall stood above it all like it had been there before the river and meant to be there after.

Marro woke with his right hand throbbing in its splints. Two fingers, bound straight to a strip of ash wood, and they itched like the bones were knitting with ants. He flexed what he could. The thumb worked. The rest of the hand was an opinion.

Four silver stags in his purse. He'd counted them twice in the dark. Counting didn't make more of them.

Rickard was already up, sitting on an upturned grain cask outside the command tent with his left arm strapped across his chest. He was trying to buckle his sword belt one-handed and losing. The burn scar on his jaw went white when he clenched it.

"Squire," he said, without looking up. "If you're going to stand there, stand there holding this."

Marro took the buckle. Rickard's shirt hung open at the collar, and a leather cord showed against his throat, dark with sweat. Rickard tugged the shirt shut over it with his good hand, quick, the way a man brushes off a fly.

"Malaquo's barges are late," Rickard said. "Third day. The men will start eating their boots by the week's end, and then they'll start eating the paymaster." A dry twitch of the mouth. "I'd sell tickets."

Down the line, Sergeant Hesse was crouched by Ash's foreleg, her patch turned toward the camp so she could watch the horse with her good eye. She ran a hand down the swollen cannon bone and swore at it, long and inventive, by the Black Wall and several things lower. Ash shifted his weight off the leg and blew.

Then Marro saw them.

Two men in grey at the edge of the picket line, where the camp followers sold onions and bad wine. Not buying. Just standing, hands folded, watching the command tent the way crows watch a cart that's about to tip. One of them caught Marro's eye and lifted two fingers to his brow, polite as a priest.

Pallo's men. Four days early, and in the company's own camp.

Marro's stomach went small and cold. Behind him, Rickard grunted as the buckle bit home.

"Friends of yours?" Rickard said. He'd seen them too. Rickard saw most things.

Hesse had stopped swearing at the horse.
