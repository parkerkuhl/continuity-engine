"How much is in it?"

Jory grinned like he'd been waiting for the question all morning. He tipped the pot out across the barrelhead with the flat of his hand and spread it so the brazier caught the silver.

"Dunk just fed it his last three. Call it twenty-two stags, and a fistful of coppers that won't buy you a whore's smile." He scooped the coppers aside. "Twenty-two. Clean silver. Some of it's even Volantene."

Dunk Two-Stones sat back on his cask, empty-handed, and rubbed his face. "Take it, then," he said thickly. "Somebody take it. I can't look at it."

Somebody pushed the cup at Marro. A battered leather thing, the bones rattling inside it. "One throw won't hurt," said a red-haired lad whose name Marro didn't know. "Stag to cast. You match the caster's point or you don't. Gods do the rest."

Twenty-two. Twenty-two and his four was twenty-six. He'd still be four short of Pallo. Four stags. Pallo might take twenty-six and a promise. Pallo might take twenty-six and his other hand.

Marro took the cup. He hadn't decided anything. His left hand had simply closed on it, and the leather was warm from Dunk's grip.

"House rules," the Braavosi said from his corner. It was the first time he'd spoken. His voice was soft, his Common careful, each word set down like a coin on a counting table. "A man who casts must cover what he wishes to win. You wish the whole pot, you put up the whole pot. Or the part you can cover." He turned his ring. "You have four, I think. Four wins you four."

So that was the game. Four would win four. Eight wouldn't pay Pallo. Twenty-two would need twenty-two up front, and nobody here was going to lend a squire with broken fingers eighteen stags against his own throw.

Unless somebody was.

The Braavosi was still smiling. "Or," he said, "a man might put up something that is not coin."

Jory laughed a little too loudly. Dunk didn't laugh at all. Outside, somebody down the horse lines was calling Marro's name. It sounded like Hesse.
