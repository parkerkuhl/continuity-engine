Tobbin was still waiting on his answer, gloves turning in his hands, when the runner came through the fog.

It was Rickard's tent-boy, Cass, barefoot and out of breath. "Captain wants the squire. Now, he says."

Rickard hadn't moved from the grain crate. The cup was empty. Marro looked past him at the Black Wall, a dark line behind the fog, and the first thing he thought of was the inside of it. Silk awnings. Old Blood eating off silver while the company chewed weevils. Rickard's mother's people, somewhere behind all that fused stone.

Rickard saw where he was looking. His grey eyes went flat.

"Whatever you're chewing on, swallow it," he said, low. "There's no road through that wall for the likes of us. No pass, no bridge, no friends. If there were, I wouldn't be sitting in mud." He tipped the cup over and watched the dregs run out. "And what I told you stays between you and me. Not Hesse. Not anyone."

Then, at his usual volume: "Did you tell her about the wagon road?"

Behind them, near the horse lines, Tobbin stood where Marro had left him, watching. The man in grey had gone from the fence post. Marro didn't see where.

Rickard was waiting.
