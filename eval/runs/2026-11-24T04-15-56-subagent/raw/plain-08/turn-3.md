Hesse left Ash and came across the mud with her bowlegged sergeant's walk. She stopped at Marro's shoulder, close enough that he could smell horse liniment and old smoke on her, and dropped her voice so it went no further than his ear.

"What's the knight want with you? Face on him like a wet funeral."

"I don't know," Marro said.

Her eye stayed on him a beat too long. She'd known him since he was small enough to hide in a grain sack, and she'd seen him lie to dice-house men and quartermasters and once to a Triarch's tax-taker. She knew how it looked.

"Huh," she said. That was all.

Rickard watched them both from the crate. He'd heard none of it, and he didn't like that.

"Sergeant. Six men on the wagon road, Malaquo's barges are late again. Pick ones who can keep their tempers." He looked at Marro. "You. Inside. I want my arm redressed and I want to talk."

The tent flap hung open behind him. Inside, on the camp table, sat the iron-bound strongbox Rickard never let out of his sight.

Tobbin hadn't moved from the edge of the horse lines.
