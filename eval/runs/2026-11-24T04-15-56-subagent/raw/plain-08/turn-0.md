**Chapter 2: Grey Men**

The fog came up off the Rhoyne before dawn and sat on the camp like wet wool. Marro woke with his right hand throbbing in its splints, the two bad fingers fat and hot, and lay a while listening to the cookfires spit.

Four days. Thirty stags. He had four.

He didn't need to count them again. He counted them anyway.

Rickard was already up, sitting on an upturned grain crate outside the command tent with his left arm strapped to his chest and a cup of something steaming in his right. The burn scar on his jaw looked grey in that light. He watched the fog and didn't look at Marro.

"Malaquo's barges were late," he said. "Third time this month. Go tell Hesse to put six men on the wagon road. If the Triarch wants to starve us he can do it to our faces."

The horse lines stank of wet straw. Hesse stood over Ash with a hoof in her hand and her patch pushed up on her forehead to scratch beneath it, then pulled it back down when she saw him. The mare's off foreleg was wrapped again.

"She's no better," Hesse said. "Gods' teeth, don't look at her like that. Say what he wants."

Marro had his mouth open to say it when someone said his name behind him.

Ser Tobbin came through the fog with his cloak dark at the hem and a long face that had not slept. He was a big man, slow-spoken, with his sister's narrow nose. Marro had not noticed that before. He noticed it now.

"You're the captain's boy. You were in the dock alleys a week back, the night of the brawl. Folk saw you." Tobbin turned his gloves over in his hands. "My sister kept a stall down there. Dagna. She's not been seen since that night. I'm going down to ask after her tonight, and I want someone with me who knows the turnings."

Beyond him, at the edge of the lines where the fog thinned, a man in a grey cloak was leaning on a fence post, doing nothing at all.

Hesse's one eye went from Tobbin to Marro and stayed there.
