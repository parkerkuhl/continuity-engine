The order of march never got finished.

"He knows before we do." A young captain near the tent flap, one of the Lyseni, said it to nobody and everybody. "We decided on the eastern bridge two days ago. Yesterday they doubled it. That's not a market spy. That's somebody at this table."

Heads turned. Not all of them toward Rickard. But enough.

Marro felt it before he understood it, the way you feel a crowd shift in a dice-house before the first stool goes over. Rickard was the one with the bolt in his shoulder. Rickard was the one who'd argued for the eastern bridge. Rickard had walked the Black Wall alone, twice, to look at the gate. Everyone at the table knew all three things. Marro watched them remember.

Rickard didn't move. He let the silence go on a beat too long, and Marro, standing behind him with the helm pressed to his ribs, couldn't tell if it was nerve or something else.

"Say it, then," Rickard said.

The Lyseni flushed. "I'm saying nothing. I'm asking who's been near the wall."

"I have." Rickard turned his head, slow, so the burn scar caught the tallow light. "So have half your men, buying wine they can't afford through the gate grates. So has the paymaster, counting Malaquo's sacks." He didn't look at Lysono when he said it. He didn't need to. "You want a rat, Danno, start with the ones who can count."

Lysono's pen stopped.

The commander's voice cut through. "Enough. Every captain keeps his own tent tonight. Nobody leaves the lines without my word."

Chairs scraped. The council broke up into knots and mutters. Rickard stood, grunted when his left arm took a fraction of the weight, and reached back for his helm without looking.

Over his shoulder, very quiet: "Walk with me, boy."

At the flap, Ser Tobbin stood waiting for someone, arms crossed. He was watching Marro.
