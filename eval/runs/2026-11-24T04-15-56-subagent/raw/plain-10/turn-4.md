Rickard stopped at the flap. Turned back to the room, where the knots of captains had gone quiet to listen.

"My shield," he said. Loud enough for the whole pavilion.

It hung on the tent pole behind his chair, the company's gold skulls chipped on the face. Marro went for it. He had to hook it off the peg with his left hand and hug it against his chest, helm still under the same arm, the splinted fingers of his right held clear. Clumsy. Somebody's squire snickered.

"First men on the eastern bridge," Rickard said to the commander, "go under my banner. I'll lead them myself. Then let Danno tell me who sold the plan."

Marro held the shield out.

Rickard took it with his right hand, and for a breath it was fine. Then he tried to pass it across to the left arm, where a shield belongs, and get the forearm through the straps.

The arm came up as far as his ribs and stopped. Marro saw the muscle in his jaw jump, saw the scar go white. The shield's rim knocked against his knee. He tried again and got the strap halfway over his wrist, and the arm simply would not lift it. It hung there, the shield dragging down toward the floor, held up by the one strap and his clenched right hand.

Nobody laughed this time.

The commander looked at the shield for a long moment. "Rickard. You can't raise that."

"I can carry it."

"Carry it into a bridge fight and you'll carry it into the river." The old man's voice wasn't unkind. That made it worse. "Your men go with Danno. You hold the reserve."

Rickard's grey eyes went flat. He shrugged the strap off his wrist and held the shield out sideways, toward Marro, without looking at him.

Close enough that only Marro heard: "You saw nothing. Get my things."

Behind them, Ser Tobbin had stepped into the flap, blocking the way out.

"Squire," Tobbin said. "A word about the eastern alleys."
