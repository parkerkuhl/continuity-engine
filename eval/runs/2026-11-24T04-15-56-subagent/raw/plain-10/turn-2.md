It was the forked beard who asked it, leaning back until his chair creaked.

"Here's what I'd like explained. Malaquo is a Triarch of this city. We're camped under his walls. And he sells us grain on credit. Two months of it." He spread his hands. "Why does a man feed the wolf at his own door?"

"Because the other two Triarchs want him dead more than they want us gone," the commander said. "Politics. He wants us hungry enough to need him and fed enough to stay."

"Or he wants us right where we are," said the forked beard.

Marro kept his mouth shut and his eyes on Rickard.

The captain's face didn't do much. It never did. The burn scar along his jaw stayed pale and still. But his right thumb had found the edge of the table and was pressing into the wood, slow, the way he pressed on a wound to see if it still hurt. And he wasn't looking at the forked beard. He was looking at the map, at the Long Bridge, as if the question had been asked of someone else.

Then he looked up and smiled with half his mouth.

"Malaquo feeds us because we owe him," Rickard said. "A man you owe is a man you own. Ask any squire with dice in his purse." A few captains laughed.

The words went into Marro like a cold nail. He knew the joke wasn't for him. It sat on him anyway, Pallo's grey men and the new moon and four stags in a purse that should hold thirty.

The forked beard didn't laugh. "Then who owns us?"

"Malaquo," Rickard said, easy. "Until we pay him in his own city."

The commander rapped the table and moved on to the order of march. Rickard's thumb came off the wood. There was a little pale dent where it had been.

Across the pavilion, behind the commander's chair, Lysono Qhoran was writing something down.
