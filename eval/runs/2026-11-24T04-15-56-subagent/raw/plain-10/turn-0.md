**Chapter 2: The Long Bridge**

Morning came grey and wet off the river. The camp smelled of smoke and latrine trenches and bread the company hadn't paid for.

Marro sat on an upturned bucket outside Rickard's tent and worked the whetstone along a sword that wasn't his. Left hand. Slow. The splinted fingers of his right hand rested on his knee like something borrowed. Every stroke he got wrong, he felt it in the knuckles.

Four stags in his purse. Thirty owed. He'd done the sum enough times that it had stopped being a sum and become a weight.

Inside the tent, Rickard was talking low with someone. Marro caught the captain's voice, flat and dry, then a second voice, softer. Lysono Qhoran. The paymaster didn't come to captains' tents at dawn to talk about the weather.

"...by the new moon," Lysono said. "The Triarch's factor counts the sacks himself now."

Rickard said something Marro couldn't make out. Then, clearer: "Tell him he'll get his grain money when he gets his city."

The flap opened. Lysono stepped out, neat as a ledger, and paused. His eyes went to the splint, then to Marro's face.

"Squire," he said. "You were in the eastern alleys some nights ago. A woman named Dagna went missing there. Her brother has been asking." A small pause. "Ser Tobbin is asking me for leave to go looking. I thought you might have seen something."

Down the line of tents, Hesse was leading Ash toward the farrier, the horse favoring its bad leg, her patch turned toward the river. She hadn't seen him yet.

Lysono waited, hands folded.
