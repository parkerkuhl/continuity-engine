Lysono had let the question hang, then let it go. "Later, then," he'd said, and walked off toward the paymaster's wagon without looking back. That was worse than if he'd pressed.

The big pavilion stank of wet canvas and tallow. Eight captains around a trestle table, a map of Volantis weighted at the corners with daggers and a wine cup. Squires and sworn men stood behind their masters along the walls, holding helms and cloaks and their tongues.

Marro stood behind Rickard with the captain's helm tucked against his ribs, left arm doing the work. The right hand stayed down, out of sight.

Rickard sat stiff in his chair. He kept the left arm close, elbow pinned to his side, and when he leaned to point at the map he did it with his right. Marro saw the sweat at his temple. Nobody else would. Or everybody would, and nobody would say.

The company's commander, an old man with a voice like gravel in a bucket, tapped the map at the Long Bridge.

"Triarch's doubled the guard on the eastern end. Again." He looked around the table. "Third time they've moved before we have. Somebody tell me how."

Silence. A captain with a forked beard picked at the wax on the table.

"Spies in the market," someone offered.

"Spies in this tent," said the forked beard, not looking up.

Rickard laughed once, short. "If there's a rat, he's got a sense of humor. Leaves us the grain and takes the plans."

The commander didn't laugh. "We go at the bridge in three nights. Nobody outside this tent hears it." His eyes went along the wall, along the squires. They stopped on Marro for a moment, on the arm he was keeping behind his back, then moved on.

Rickard's hand came back, palm up, without turning around. He wanted the map case on the stool beside Marro. The one with the strap you needed two hands to buckle.
