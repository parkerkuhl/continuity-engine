That landed. Benches creaked. Somebody laughed and stopped.

The captain's face didn't move. That was the first thing. Rickard had a good face for councils, all scar and patience. But his right hand had come up off his knee, slow, like a man brushing at a fly, and settled at his collar. On the cord. Thumb on the cord, through the cloth, where the key hung. He held it there for the space of a breath and then let the hand fall, easy, and said, "Because a Triarch thinks further than a ditch. He wants us hungry and grateful, not gone. Credit's a leash."

It was a decent answer. Two captains nodded.

Marro's stomach had gone cold and tight anyway. He'd seen that hand before, on other men, at the dice table, when they had a bad throw under the cup and were praying nobody lifted it.

He looked away too late.

Lysono Qhoran was watching him. Not Rickard. Him. The paymaster's pen had stopped over the ledger, and his eyes went from Marro's face to Rickard's collar and back, and then he dipped the pen and wrote something short.

"Credit is a leash," Lysono agreed pleasantly. "And leashes have two ends."

Down the table, Tobbin was still standing. "I asked the squire a question," he said.
