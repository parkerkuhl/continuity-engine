"Shield," Rickard said, before Tobbin could say the name again. "The first assault, I lead it myself. Let the wall see whose plan it is."

It cut the room clean in half. Half the captains thumped the table. Tobbin's mouth closed on whatever he'd been about to say.

Marro set the helm down on the stool's end and fetched the shield from where it leaned by the tent pole. Oak and iron, the company's black skulls on gold gone dull with river damp. Heavy enough that his splinted hand complained just lifting the edge. He stepped up and held it out to Rickard's left side, the way he'd done a hundred mornings.

Rickard's left arm didn't move.

It couldn't. It sat bound against his chest where the bolt had gone in, and the strap that should have taken the shield's weight hung there with nothing to take. For a moment the two of them stood like that, the squire holding out the shield and the captain looking at it, and the whole pavilion saw it.

Then Rickard reached across with his right hand, took the shield by its rim, and set it down against his own knee. "When the arm's healed," he said, pleasant, as if he'd meant to do exactly that.

Nobody thumped the table now.

"When," said Lysono Qhoran, and wrote.

The grey-bearded captain cleared his throat and said the assault could wait for the plan in any case, and that was the end of it. The benches emptied in twos and threes. Tobbin went out last but one, and at the flap he turned and found Marro across the trampled rushes.

"Tonight," he said. "You and me. We'll talk about the river."

He let the canvas fall behind him.

Rickard sat with the shield against his knee and didn't look at anyone. His thumb had found the cord at his collar again.

Hesse came off her tent pole and stood beside Marro, close, her one eye on the flap where Tobbin had gone. "You've got the day," she said, low. "Rickard's errand, or Tobbin's talk, or Pallo's grey men. You won't get all three."
