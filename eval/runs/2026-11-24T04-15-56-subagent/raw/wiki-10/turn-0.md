**Chapter 2: Four Days**

The fog sat on the Rhoyne like wet wool, and the camp woke coughing into it.

Marro was up before the drums. He always was now. The splinted fingers throbbed when the cold got into them, and the cold had got into everything. He worked the whetstone along the cudgel's iron band for something to do with his good hand. You couldn't sharpen a cudgel. He did it anyway.

Four days to the new moon. Thirty stags. He had four. The arithmetic didn't improve with looking.

Hesse came down the picket line leading Ash, who walked like an old man on a bad stair. The patch over her left eye was dark with damp. She stopped beside him and watched the wall, not him.

"They doubled the torches on the Black Wall last night," she said. "Counted them from the ditch. Forty where there were twenty."

"Maybe they're cold too."

"Maybe." She scratched Ash under the jaw. "The grain barges didn't come either. Triarch's credit, Triarch's barges. Funny thing, a creditor going shy."

Across the mud, the flap of Rickard's tent opened. The captain came out with his left arm strapped to his chest and his face grey under the burn scar. He looked older than he had a week ago. He saw Marro and lifted his chin: *come here.*

Marro went.

Inside, the tent smelled of tallow and old blood. A strongbox sat at the foot of the cot. Rickard eased himself onto a stool and didn't sit down so much as stop falling.

"I need someone across the river," he said. "Into the east city. Quiet. A message to a man in the Dragon's Den, and an answer back before nightfall." He looked at Marro's hand, then his face. "Bridge is shut. You'll have to find another way over. Can you do it?"

The Dragon's Den. Lissa's mother lived there. The letter sat folded against Marro's ribs, under the borrowed cloak.

Outside, Hesse had stopped walking Ash. She stood just past the tent flap, close enough to hear.
