"He knows them before we've made them," said a younger captain with a Lysene braid. "The ford at the south bend, a fortnight back. They were waiting with pitch. We'd decided it that same night, in this tent."

Then they were all looking at Rickard. Not accusing. Not yet. Just looking at the man whose plans they were.

Rickard let them. He took his time about it, the way he did with a horse that wanted to bolt, and when he spoke his voice was flat and tired and reasonable.

"Then there's a mouth in this camp," he said. "Could be a cook. Could be a whore who hears a captain brag. Could be one of you." He let that sit. "So the next plan doesn't come to this table. I'll draw it and hold it, and you'll get your orders the morning you march and not an hour before."

Grumbling, but the grumbling of men who'd heard worse ideas.

"Hold it yourself," Lysono said, not looking up from his ledger. "In the strongbox, I imagine. Very safe." The pen scratched. "I will want to see it, captain. The company pays for these plans. The company reads them."

"When they're done."

"Of course."

Marro had the helm against his chest now, both arms around it like a shield he wasn't allowed to carry. He could feel the shape of the thing in the room, the way you feel a step in the dark before your foot finds it. He didn't have a name for it. He didn't want one.

Ser Tobbin hadn't sat down. He'd waited out the whole exchange with his fists on the table and his red eyes on Marro, and now the talk had moved on without him, which only made it worse.

"Squire," he said again, quieter. "My sister."

Hesse straightened off the tent pole.
