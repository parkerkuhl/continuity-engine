The house was a mile off and across the water, and the line didn't care how badly he wanted to knock on its door.

Four ahead. Three. The fishwife had gone off cursing toward the river stairs, rubbing her ribs. An old man in a wine-merchant's coat handed up his paper, and the clerk held it to the light, turned it over, pressed a thumbnail to the wax to see if it was fresh. He took his time about it. The old man paid him a coin that wasn't on any tariff, and the spearmen let him through.

So there was a price. Whether four stags covered it, and whether a clerk would take silver from a sellsword's squire with no paper at all, was another matter. The Golden Company was camped outside these people's walls. Marro's borrowed cloak had the company's colour at the hem, faded but there.

Two ahead.

Under the first arch the skiff boy had found no fares and was drifting back toward the reeds, idle, one hand trailing in the brown water. A pair of the Triarch's men watched the river from a balcony over the arch. They didn't seem to be watching him in particular. They didn't seem to be not watching him either.

Lissa's letter was warm against his ribs. Three days to the new moon.

The clerk looked up past the woman in front of Marro and found him with flat, bored eyes.

"Paper," he said, before it was even Marro's turn.
