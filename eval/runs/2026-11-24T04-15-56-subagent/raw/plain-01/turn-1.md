Rickard had folded Lysono's paper into his belt the night before and said nothing more about it. That was its own kind of answer, and Marro hadn't liked it.

Dawn came grey and cold. Three days to the new moon now.

There was one horse between them. Rickard's bay, a long-legged gelding with a temper. Ash was still standing hipshot in the lines with her bad foreleg cocked, and the company had nothing spare, not even a mule. So Rickard rode and Marro walked at his stirrup, the cudgel in his left hand because the right was no good for it. It felt wrong there. Like writing with your foot.

Rickard rode one-handed, reins gathered in his right, the strapped arm tucked against his ribs. He couldn't have raised a shield if a crossbowman stood up in front of him. They both knew it. Neither of them said it.

The ford lay a mile south, where the Rhoyne spread out shallow and brown over gravel. Reeds grew thick along both banks, taller than a man, rattling in the wind off the water.

Marro watched them.

Halfway down the near bank, the reeds were wrong. A strip of them leaned the other way from the wind, bent and not sprung back. Something had gone through there, and recently. Low in the mud at the water's edge he saw it: a boot print, heel deep, toe pointing inland. Then another. Hobnails. Volantene soldiers wore hobnails. Sellswords wore whatever they'd taken off the last dead man.

He'd been looking so hard at the mud that he'd stopped watching the far bank.

A bird went up out of the reeds across the ford. Then a flash. Small and quick, the sun catching something polished. A helm, maybe. A spyglass. It was gone before he could be sure.

Rickard had seen it too. He'd gone very still in the saddle, the way he did at dice when the other man showed a good hand.

"Well," he said quietly. "Someone's up early." He didn't turn his head. "They'll have seen the horse. They'll have seen you."

The bay shifted under him and blew. The wind changed, and for a moment Marro could hear, faint across the water, the drums from the Black Wall, still going.

Rickard looked down at him. The grey eyes were hard to read.

"Your eyes are younger than mine. What do you want to do?"

The hobnailed prints ran inland, toward the camp.
