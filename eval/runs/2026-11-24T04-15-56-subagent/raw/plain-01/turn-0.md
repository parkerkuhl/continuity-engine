**Chapter 2: Grey Men**

The camp woke to the sound of someone else's drums.

They came across the water from the Black Wall, slow and heavy, and they hadn't been there yesterday. Marro lay under the borrowed cloak and listened to them and tried to make them mean nothing. His right hand throbbed inside its splints. Two fingers, bound straight to a stick of ash. He could hold a cup with it. He could not hold anything that mattered.

Four stags in his purse. He counted them anyway, by feel, with the left hand. Still four.

Outside, the mud had gone stiff overnight. Cookfires smoked under wet wood. A line of men stood at the grain cart, and the quartermaster was ladling out less than last week, and nobody said Malaquo's name, which meant everybody was thinking it.

Hesse found him at the latrine trench. She looked worse in daylight. The patch sat crooked over the empty socket, and the skin around its edge was the color of a plum.

"Seven hells, you look like a drowned dog." She jerked her chin toward the river. "Count the walls."

He looked. On the Black Wall, where there had been one torch every forty paces last night, there were two now. Men moved between them. More men than the wall needed for watching.

"Drums started at dawn," Hesse said. "Somebody over there thinks they know something." She spat. "Ash is still lame. If they come at us, I'm on foot like the rest of you. Captain wants you. Now, not after you've finished admiring the view."

Rickard's tent smelled of vinegar and old blood. He sat on his cot with his left arm strapped across his chest, shirt open, a dressing gone brown at the shoulder. The burn scar on his jaw looked waxy in the lamplight. The cord at his neck had slipped out of his collar, a small iron key at the end of it. He tucked it back without looking at it.

"Squire." The grey eyes went to the splinted hand, then away. "Can you still write with that?"

"Left-handed. Badly."

"Badly will do." He nodded at a folded paper on the strongbox at the foot of the cot. "Lysono's been at the books. He wants to know who's been drawing silver against my name. He thinks it might be you." A pause, dry as a bone. "I told him you couldn't afford to steal. Was I right?"

Behind Marro, the tent flap stirred. Somewhere past the horse lines a man in grey stood at the edge of the camp, not doing anything, just standing where Marro would see him on the way out.

The paper sat on the strongbox. Rickard waited.
