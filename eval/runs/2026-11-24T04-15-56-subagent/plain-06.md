**Chapter 2: Grey Men**

The camp woke to the smell of wet ash and somebody else's bacon.

Marro sat on an upturned grain barrel behind the horse lines and worked Rickard's old whetstone along the cudgel's iron cap. There was nothing on it to sharpen. His right hand lay in his lap, two fingers bound to a stick with strips of shirt, throbbing in time with his pulse. The stone gave his left hand something to do. Four silver stags sat in his purse, and his mind went back to them every time he stopped moving.

Thirty, by the new moon. He had four.

Across the lines Hesse knelt in the mud at Ash's off foreleg, her head turned all the way round so her good eye could see what her hands were doing. The patch sat crooked under her hood. The mare shifted her weight off the bad leg and blew.

"Swelling's down," Hesse said, to the horse or to him. "Seven hells. Down's not gone."

Two men came along the picket rope from the road. They wore grey, plain wool, cut well, and both of them had clean boots. Nobody in the camp had clean boots.

They stopped a few paces off. The shorter one smiled at Marro the way you'd smile at a dog you meant to buy.

"Squire Marro. Master Pallo sends his greetings, and his reminder." He looked at the splinted hand, then at the cudgel. "He also sends an offer. Four days is long when you've no hope of the coin. He asks only a small thing instead. Your captain keeps a strongbox."

Hesse had gone very still at the horse's leg.

The grey man waited.

## Turn 1

**Player:** Late at night I bring Rickard his wine and sit to sharpen my cudgel's iron cap with the whetstone he gave me.

**GM:**

The grey men hadn't pushed that morning. The short one let the silence run, then said, "Think on it," and they went back up the road the way they'd come, their boots still clean. Hesse finished with Ash's leg and never once asked him about it. Somehow that was worse.

By full dark the rain had thinned to a drizzle that ticked on the tent canvas. Rickard sat on his camp stool by a single tallow candle with his left arm strapped across his chest, the bandage gone yellow at the edges. He took the wine cup in his right hand without looking up from the map pinned under his boot.

"Watered," he said.

"It's what Lysono sent."

"Lysono would water the Rhoyne." He drank anyway.

Marro sat on the chest at the foot of the cot and laid the cudgel across his knees. With the splinted hand he could only pin the shaft, clumsy, the bound fingers sticking out like a broken fork. The whetstone was in his left. It scraped. It sounded wrong on iron that had never held an edge, and he kept at it.

Rickard watched him for a while over the rim of the cup.

"You'll wear that stone to a pebble on a club."

The candle guttered. Under the open collar of Rickard's shirt a leather cord showed against the burn-pale skin of his throat, and something small and heavy hung on it, dark against his chest. Marro had seen it a hundred times. Tonight his eye kept going back to it. *Your captain keeps a strongbox.*

The strongbox sat under the cot, a foot behind Marro's heels.

Outside, someone ran past along the tent row, then a second set of feet, and a voice he didn't know said something low about the east ditch. Rickard's head turned toward the flap. He didn't get up.

"Plenty of traffic for a wet night," he said. He set the cup down. "Pour me another, if you're done grinding."

## Turn 2

**Player:** I ask him whether he meant it, about making me a knight.

**GM:**

Rickard didn't answer straight off. He held the cup out, and Marro had to set the cudgel down and pour left-handed, slopping a little on the map. Rickard watched the stain spread across the ink of the eastern wall and didn't wipe it.

"I said it if the company takes the city," he said. "I remember what I said."

"That's not what I asked."

The scar on his jaw pulled when he smiled. It wasn't much of a smile. "No. It isn't." He drank. "You want an oath? Fine. By the Black Wall, the day we're through that gate I'll put a sword on your shoulder in front of the whole company, and Lysono can write it in his little books." He tipped the cup at Marro's hand. "Though you'll need fingers that close round the hilt before anyone calls you ser."

It was the most words he'd strung together in a week. Marro knew the voice Rickard used for the men before a fight, easy and sure, and this was close to it. Close. The grey eyes had gone back to the flap while he spoke, to the dark where the running feet had gone.

Something in Marro's chest lifted at *ser* and hung there, unsure whether to trust the hook it was on.

"Land of my own," Rickard said, half to himself. "Squire with a knighthood. Pretty pair of dreams for two men sleeping in mud." He set the cup on the stool and rubbed his bad shoulder with the heel of his hand, and the cord at his throat shifted. "If I didn't mean it I'd have told you so. I'm not a kind enough man to lie for your comfort."

He looked back at Marro then, steady.

"Why ask tonight?"

## Turn 3

**Player:** While he sleeps I look for the letter he got from the Triarch. Where would he keep it?

**GM:**

Rickard slept the way wounded men did, on his back because he couldn't lie on the shoulder, breathing through his mouth. The candle was down to a stub. Rain ticked on the canvas.

Marro hadn't seen any letter from a Triarch. Nobody had told him there was one. But if Rickard kept anything he didn't want the company reading, Marro could count the places on one hand, and he'd been fetching and folding in this tent long enough to know them.

He went through them quietly, left-handed, the splint held up off everything like a sore paw.

He made no sound. The map case held maps, the eastern wall and the Long Bridge drawn three times over in Rickard's cramped hand, the third with marks Marro didn't understand. The satchel by the stool held company chits, a pay list with Lysono's neat initials down the margin, a bit of cheese gone hard. The boots held nothing. Nothing under the bedroll but straw.

That left the strongbox under the cot, a foot from Rickard's hanging hand. Iron-banded oak, the size of a bread loaf. Marro eased it an inch toward him and it was heavy, and it didn't rattle like coin. Paper, maybe. Or nothing. The lock was a good one, Pentoshi, the kind a cudgel would open only by waking the whole camp.

The key was on the cord at Rickard's throat. Marro had known that before he started looking.

Up close he could see it in the last of the candle, a short black iron key lying in the hollow of Rickard's collarbone, rising and falling. The knot at the back of the cord sat under his neck, under the weight of his head. To get it off, you'd have to cut it or lift him.

Rickard's breath caught, snagged on something in a dream, and went on.

The candle had maybe a quarter-hour left in it.
