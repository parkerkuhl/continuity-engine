**Chapter 2: The Long Bridge**

The camp woke the way it always did, with somebody else's cough.

Marro lay under the borrowed cloak and listened to it. Then the drums from the Black Wall, slow, the Triarch's men changing the watch up on the stones. The ditch between the camp and the city had gone green overnight. Spring, the old sellswords said, was when the fevers came back for second helpings. Brother Olyvar would have had something to say about that. Brother Olyvar was under three feet of mud by the wall, and the company still hadn't found anyone else who could do a burial right.

His right hand throbbed under the splints. Two fingers, bound stiff to a sliver of tent pole. He could hold a spoon. He could not hold a blade, which was a joke, because he had no blade to hold. The dagger was somewhere at the bottom of the Rhoyne with Dagna. He didn't let himself follow that thought down. Four silver stags in his purse. Four days to the new moon. Thirty owed.

The sums didn't get better for being done twice.

Outside the captain's tent, Rickard Snow was trying to buckle his own sword belt one-handed. His left arm hung strapped against his chest, the bolt wound still seeping yellow through the linen. He gave up halfway and stood there with the belt dangling, looking at the city like it had insulted his mother.

"Squire." He didn't turn. "You look like a man who slept in a ditch."

"Near enough, ser."

"Not ser yet." The burn scar on his jaw pulled when he almost smiled. Then the smile went away. "Lysono wants the captains in his tent at noon. All of them. Somebody's been counting supply wagons on the river road, and it isn't us." He finally looked at Marro, grey eyes flat. "The Triarch's riders hit the forage party last night. They knew which road. They knew the hour."

Down the row of tents, Hesse was crouched by Ash, running her hand along the grey's bad foreleg. The patch over her left eye was new leather; the old one had rotted through. She caught Marro looking and jerked her chin at him once. Come here, that meant. Or maybe, stay away. With Hesse it could be either until she opened her mouth.

And past her, at the edge of the horse lines, three men in grey cloaks stood waiting by the road. Not company men. Pallo's men. One of them lifted a hand, friendly as a priest.

Four days, Marro thought. They weren't supposed to come for four days.

Rickard followed his look. His eyes narrowed. "Friends of yours?"

## Turn 1

**Player:** Ser Tobbin, a hedge knight who sells his sword to the company, stops me by the cook fires. He looks like he hasn't slept.

**GM:**

The cook fires were mostly smoke and a kettle of barley that had been barley three days running. Ser Tobbin stepped out of the smoke like he'd been standing in it on purpose.

He was a big man gone loose at the edges, mail shirt rusting at the hem, a hedge knight's shield slung on his back with the paint flaking off whatever it used to be. His eyes were red-rimmed. He hadn't shaved. He smelled of wine that hadn't done its job.

"Squire. Rickard's boy." He put a hand on Marro's shoulder, heavy, and kept it there. "You go into the city. Don't say you don't. Half the camp's seen you coming back over the ditch at dawn."

Marro's mouth went dry. The splinted fingers ached all at once, like they'd heard their name.

"My sister." Tobbin's voice dropped. "Dagna. Runs cargo on the river side, the eastern quays. Small woman, sharp face, laughs like a gull. She was to meet me six nights back with wine and news. She didn't come." He swallowed. "Dagna doesn't miss a meeting. Not for weather, not for the Triarch, not for nothing. She'd crawl."

Six nights. Marro could count it too. The alley. The water. The weight of her going over.

"Somebody saw her near the dice-houses that last night." Tobbin's thumb dug into the hollow of Marro's shoulder, not quite hard enough to call it a threat. "Same night you got your hand broke, they tell me. Same street." He looked down at the splints, then back up. "So I'm asking. Kindly, while it's still kindly. You see her? You hear anything?"

Across the fire, a cook stopped stirring to listen, then pretended he hadn't.

Tobbin waited. His hand stayed where it was.

"I don't know where she is."

It came out level. Marro was almost proud of it, and then sick at being proud of it.

Tobbin searched his face for a long moment. Whatever he was looking for, he didn't find it, or found it and didn't know what it was. The hand on Marro's shoulder loosened. Then, worse, it patted him. Twice. The way you'd pat a dog that hadn't bitten you.

"Aye. Course you don't." He rubbed his eyes with the heel of his palm. "A week. Seven nights now, near enough, and I keep thinking she's laid up somewhere with a bad tooth, cursing me for not coming." A laugh that wasn't one. "She'd say I worry like a septa."

The cook went back to stirring.

"Here's the thing, squire." Tobbin bent closer. His breath was sour. "You go over the ditch. I can't. They know my face on the quays; I broke a Volantene's jaw there last year and the Triarch's watch hasn't forgot it. But you, you're nobody. Small and quick and nobody." He dug in his belt pouch and came up with something and pressed it into Marro's good hand. A copper ring, cheap, stamped with a gull. "That's hers. Matching pair, our mother left them. Show it round the eastern quays. Somebody'll know her. Somebody'll have seen."

The ring was warm from his pouch.

"I'll pay you," Tobbin said. "When I'm paid. Find her for me, and I won't forget it." He straightened. "And if somebody's done her harm, you find me that too. I won't forget that either."

He walked off toward the horse lines without waiting for an answer, shoulders hunched, past the three men in grey still loitering by the road. One of them was watching Marro now. Not Tobbin. Marro.

The ring sat in his palm. Dagna's twin was somewhere in the Rhoyne, on a finger he'd last seen sliding under black water.

## Turn 3

**Player:** He says he wants a blessing before he goes looking for her, and asks me to fetch Brother Olyvar.

**GM:**

Tobbin got ten paces before he stopped and turned back, scrubbing a hand over his stubble.

"One more thing. Before I go hunting the river for her." He looked embarrassed, which on a man his size was like watching a bear try to curtsy. "I want a blessing. Do it proper. Fetch me Brother Olyvar, would you? Tell him it's for Tobbin and I'll owe him a skin of the good red."

The cook's ladle stopped again.

Marro's stomach dropped. Olyvar. The septon's hands, yellow at the end, picking at the blanket. The mud by the Black Wall, and the men taking turns with the one good shovel because nobody wanted to be the one who finished.

"Olyvar's dead, ser," the cook said, before Marro could find his tongue. Flat, not unkind. "Fever. Ten days back. We put him in the ground out by the wall."

Tobbin stood very still.

"Dead," he said.

"You were on the night watch on the river road, ser. Most of that week."

"I was." Tobbin nodded slowly, like a man agreeing to something he didn't understand. "I was. Gods." He laughed once, short and wrong. "Olyvar. He blessed my sword when I signed the company book. Said it was the ugliest blade he'd ever prayed over." His big hands opened and closed at his sides. "There's no one else, then. No septon in the whole bloody company."

His red eyes came back to Marro. Not suspicious. Worse than that. Lost.

"You can read, can't you, squire? Rickard's always going on about it. A squire who reads." He swallowed. "Olyvar had a book. Prayers. It'll be in his things somewhere, if nobody's sold it yet." He looked at the ring still in Marro's hand. "Somebody ought to say the words for her. In case."

He didn't say in case of what.

Behind him, by the road, the grey-cloaked men had started walking. Toward the cook fires.
