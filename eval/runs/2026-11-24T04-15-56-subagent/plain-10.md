**Chapter 2: The Long Bridge**

Morning came grey and wet off the river. The camp smelled of smoke and latrine trenches and bread the company hadn't paid for.

Marro sat on an upturned bucket outside Rickard's tent and worked the whetstone along a sword that wasn't his. Left hand. Slow. The splinted fingers of his right hand rested on his knee like something borrowed. Every stroke he got wrong, he felt it in the knuckles.

Four stags in his purse. Thirty owed. He'd done the sum enough times that it had stopped being a sum and become a weight.

Inside the tent, Rickard was talking low with someone. Marro caught the captain's voice, flat and dry, then a second voice, softer. Lysono Qhoran. The paymaster didn't come to captains' tents at dawn to talk about the weather.

"...by the new moon," Lysono said. "The Triarch's factor counts the sacks himself now."

Rickard said something Marro couldn't make out. Then, clearer: "Tell him he'll get his grain money when he gets his city."

The flap opened. Lysono stepped out, neat as a ledger, and paused. His eyes went to the splint, then to Marro's face.

"Squire," he said. "You were in the eastern alleys some nights ago. A woman named Dagna went missing there. Her brother has been asking." A small pause. "Ser Tobbin is asking me for leave to go looking. I thought you might have seen something."

Down the line of tents, Hesse was leading Ash toward the farrier, the horse favoring its bad leg, her patch turned toward the river. She hadn't seen him yet.

Lysono waited, hands folded.

## Turn 1

**Player:** I stand behind Rickard at the captains' council in the big pavilion, holding his helm.

**GM:**

Lysono had let the question hang, then let it go. "Later, then," he'd said, and walked off toward the paymaster's wagon without looking back. That was worse than if he'd pressed.

The big pavilion stank of wet canvas and tallow. Eight captains around a trestle table, a map of Volantis weighted at the corners with daggers and a wine cup. Squires and sworn men stood behind their masters along the walls, holding helms and cloaks and their tongues.

Marro stood behind Rickard with the captain's helm tucked against his ribs, left arm doing the work. The right hand stayed down, out of sight.

Rickard sat stiff in his chair. He kept the left arm close, elbow pinned to his side, and when he leaned to point at the map he did it with his right. Marro saw the sweat at his temple. Nobody else would. Or everybody would, and nobody would say.

The company's commander, an old man with a voice like gravel in a bucket, tapped the map at the Long Bridge.

"Triarch's doubled the guard on the eastern end. Again." He looked around the table. "Third time they've moved before we have. Somebody tell me how."

Silence. A captain with a forked beard picked at the wax on the table.

"Spies in the market," someone offered.

"Spies in this tent," said the forked beard, not looking up.

Rickard laughed once, short. "If there's a rat, he's got a sense of humor. Leaves us the grain and takes the plans."

The commander didn't laugh. "We go at the bridge in three nights. Nobody outside this tent hears it." His eyes went along the wall, along the squires. They stopped on Marro for a moment, on the arm he was keeping behind his back, then moved on.

Rickard's hand came back, palm up, without turning around. He wanted the map case on the stool beside Marro. The one with the strap you needed two hands to buckle.

## Turn 2

**Player:** A captain asks why the Triarch would feed an army camped outside his own walls. I keep quiet and watch Rickard's face.

**GM:**

It was the forked beard who asked it, leaning back until his chair creaked.

"Here's what I'd like explained. Malaquo is a Triarch of this city. We're camped under his walls. And he sells us grain on credit. Two months of it." He spread his hands. "Why does a man feed the wolf at his own door?"

"Because the other two Triarchs want him dead more than they want us gone," the commander said. "Politics. He wants us hungry enough to need him and fed enough to stay."

"Or he wants us right where we are," said the forked beard.

Marro kept his mouth shut and his eyes on Rickard.

The captain's face didn't do much. It never did. The burn scar along his jaw stayed pale and still. But his right thumb had found the edge of the table and was pressing into the wood, slow, the way he pressed on a wound to see if it still hurt. And he wasn't looking at the forked beard. He was looking at the map, at the Long Bridge, as if the question had been asked of someone else.

Then he looked up and smiled with half his mouth.

"Malaquo feeds us because we owe him," Rickard said. "A man you owe is a man you own. Ask any squire with dice in his purse." A few captains laughed.

The words went into Marro like a cold nail. He knew the joke wasn't for him. It sat on him anyway, Pallo's grey men and the new moon and four stags in a purse that should hold thirty.

The forked beard didn't laugh. "Then who owns us?"

"Malaquo," Rickard said, easy. "Until we pay him in his own city."

The commander rapped the table and moved on to the order of march. Rickard's thumb came off the wood. There was a little pale dent where it had been.

Across the pavilion, behind the commander's chair, Lysono Qhoran was writing something down.

## Turn 3

**Player:** Someone says the Triarch seems to know our plans before we make them. The captains turn to Rickard.

**GM:**

The order of march never got finished.

"He knows before we do." A young captain near the tent flap, one of the Lyseni, said it to nobody and everybody. "We decided on the eastern bridge two days ago. Yesterday they doubled it. That's not a market spy. That's somebody at this table."

Heads turned. Not all of them toward Rickard. But enough.

Marro felt it before he understood it, the way you feel a crowd shift in a dice-house before the first stool goes over. Rickard was the one with the bolt in his shoulder. Rickard was the one who'd argued for the eastern bridge. Rickard had walked the Black Wall alone, twice, to look at the gate. Everyone at the table knew all three things. Marro watched them remember.

Rickard didn't move. He let the silence go on a beat too long, and Marro, standing behind him with the helm pressed to his ribs, couldn't tell if it was nerve or something else.

"Say it, then," Rickard said.

The Lyseni flushed. "I'm saying nothing. I'm asking who's been near the wall."

"I have." Rickard turned his head, slow, so the burn scar caught the tallow light. "So have half your men, buying wine they can't afford through the gate grates. So has the paymaster, counting Malaquo's sacks." He didn't look at Lysono when he said it. He didn't need to. "You want a rat, Danno, start with the ones who can count."

Lysono's pen stopped.

The commander's voice cut through. "Enough. Every captain keeps his own tent tonight. Nobody leaves the lines without my word."

Chairs scraped. The council broke up into knots and mutters. Rickard stood, grunted when his left arm took a fraction of the weight, and reached back for his helm without looking.

Over his shoulder, very quiet: "Walk with me, boy."

At the flap, Ser Tobbin stood waiting for someone, arms crossed. He was watching Marro.

## Turn 4

**Player:** Rickard calls for his shield and says he will lead the first assault himself. I step forward to hand it to him.

**GM:**

Rickard stopped at the flap. Turned back to the room, where the knots of captains had gone quiet to listen.

"My shield," he said. Loud enough for the whole pavilion.

It hung on the tent pole behind his chair, the company's gold skulls chipped on the face. Marro went for it. He had to hook it off the peg with his left hand and hug it against his chest, helm still under the same arm, the splinted fingers of his right held clear. Clumsy. Somebody's squire snickered.

"First men on the eastern bridge," Rickard said to the commander, "go under my banner. I'll lead them myself. Then let Danno tell me who sold the plan."

Marro held the shield out.

Rickard took it with his right hand, and for a breath it was fine. Then he tried to pass it across to the left arm, where a shield belongs, and get the forearm through the straps.

The arm came up as far as his ribs and stopped. Marro saw the muscle in his jaw jump, saw the scar go white. The shield's rim knocked against his knee. He tried again and got the strap halfway over his wrist, and the arm simply would not lift it. It hung there, the shield dragging down toward the floor, held up by the one strap and his clenched right hand.

Nobody laughed this time.

The commander looked at the shield for a long moment. "Rickard. You can't raise that."

"I can carry it."

"Carry it into a bridge fight and you'll carry it into the river." The old man's voice wasn't unkind. That made it worse. "Your men go with Danno. You hold the reserve."

Rickard's grey eyes went flat. He shrugged the strap off his wrist and held the shield out sideways, toward Marro, without looking at him.

Close enough that only Marro heard: "You saw nothing. Get my things."

Behind them, Ser Tobbin had stepped into the flap, blocking the way out.

"Squire," Tobbin said. "A word about the eastern alleys."
