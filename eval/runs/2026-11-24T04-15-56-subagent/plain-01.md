**Chapter 2: Grey Men**

The camp woke to the sound of someone else's drums.

They came across the water from the Black Wall, slow and heavy, and they hadn't been there yesterday. Marro lay under the borrowed cloak and listened to them and tried to make them mean nothing. His right hand throbbed inside its splints. Two fingers, bound straight to a stick of ash. He could hold a cup with it. He could not hold anything that mattered.

Four stags in his purse. He counted them anyway, by feel, with the left hand. Still four.

Outside, the mud had gone stiff overnight. Cookfires smoked under wet wood. A line of men stood at the grain cart, and the quartermaster was ladling out less than last week, and nobody said Malaquo's name, which meant everybody was thinking it.

Hesse found him at the latrine trench. She looked worse in daylight. The patch sat crooked over the empty socket, and the skin around its edge was the color of a plum.

"Seven hells, you look like a drowned dog." She jerked her chin toward the river. "Count the walls."

He looked. On the Black Wall, where there had been one torch every forty paces last night, there were two now. Men moved between them. More men than the wall needed for watching.

"Drums started at dawn," Hesse said. "Somebody over there thinks they know something." She spat. "Ash is still lame. If they come at us, I'm on foot like the rest of you. Captain wants you. Now, not after you've finished admiring the view."

Rickard's tent smelled of vinegar and old blood. He sat on his cot with his left arm strapped across his chest, shirt open, a dressing gone brown at the shoulder. The burn scar on his jaw looked waxy in the lamplight. The cord at his neck had slipped out of his collar, a small iron key at the end of it. He tucked it back without looking at it.

"Squire." The grey eyes went to the splinted hand, then away. "Can you still write with that?"

"Left-handed. Badly."

"Badly will do." He nodded at a folded paper on the strongbox at the foot of the cot. "Lysono's been at the books. He wants to know who's been drawing silver against my name. He thinks it might be you." A pause, dry as a bone. "I told him you couldn't afford to steal. Was I right?"

Behind Marro, the tent flap stirred. Somewhere past the horse lines a man in grey stood at the edge of the camp, not doing anything, just standing where Marro would see him on the way out.

The paper sat on the strongbox. Rickard waited.

## Turn 1

**Player:** Rickard and I ride out at dawn to scout the ford south of the camp. I keep close to him and watch the reeds.

**GM:**

Rickard had folded Lysono's paper into his belt the night before and said nothing more about it. That was its own kind of answer, and Marro hadn't liked it.

Dawn came grey and cold. Three days to the new moon now.

There was one horse between them. Rickard's bay, a long-legged gelding with a temper. Ash was still standing hipshot in the lines with her bad foreleg cocked, and the company had nothing spare, not even a mule. So Rickard rode and Marro walked at his stirrup, the cudgel in his left hand because the right was no good for it. It felt wrong there. Like writing with your foot.

Rickard rode one-handed, reins gathered in his right, the strapped arm tucked against his ribs. He couldn't have raised a shield if a crossbowman stood up in front of him. They both knew it. Neither of them said it.

The ford lay a mile south, where the Rhoyne spread out shallow and brown over gravel. Reeds grew thick along both banks, taller than a man, rattling in the wind off the water.

Marro watched them.

**Wits check: (4+2) +2 Wits = 8.** Success, with a cost.

Halfway down the near bank, the reeds were wrong. A strip of them leaned the other way from the wind, bent and not sprung back. Something had gone through there, and recently. Low in the mud at the water's edge he saw it: a boot print, heel deep, toe pointing inland. Then another. Hobnails. Volantene soldiers wore hobnails. Sellswords wore whatever they'd taken off the last dead man.

He'd been looking so hard at the mud that he'd stopped watching the far bank.

A bird went up out of the reeds across the ford. Then a flash. Small and quick, the sun catching something polished. A helm, maybe. A spyglass. It was gone before he could be sure.

Rickard had seen it too. He'd gone very still in the saddle, the way he did at dice when the other man showed a good hand.

"Well," he said quietly. "Someone's up early." He didn't turn his head. "They'll have seen the horse. They'll have seen you."

The bay shifted under him and blew. The wind changed, and for a moment Marro could hear, faint across the water, the drums from the Black Wall, still going.

Rickard looked down at him. The grey eyes were hard to read.

"Your eyes are younger than mine. What do you want to do?"

The hobnailed prints ran inland, toward the camp.

## Turn 2

**Player:** When the ambush comes I put myself between Rickard and the bowmen and shout for him to get his shield up.

**GM:**

It came before either of them had decided anything.

A man stood up out of the reeds on the near bank, thirty paces off, and then two more beside him. Short bows, already drawn. Grey cloaks, mud to the knee. Not soldiers' cloaks. Pallo's men wore grey.

The thought went through Marro and out the other side. No time to hold it.

He moved. Three strides across the gravel, cudgel up in the wrong hand, his body between the bows and the bay.

"Shield!" he shouted. "Get your shield up!"

**Finesse check: (3+5) +2 Finesse −1 outnumbered = 9.** You get there, at a cost.

The first arrow went past his ear with a sound like tearing cloth. The second took him across the outside of the left thigh, a hot line, and he felt the wet start at once. Not deep. Deep enough.

Behind him Rickard laughed. A short, ugly sound.

"With what arm, boy?"

The shield hung on its strap from the saddle-bow where it had hung all morning. Rickard's left arm was bound across his chest. He couldn't have lifted it if the Seven had come down and asked him nicely. Marro had known that. He'd known it on the walk down. He'd shouted it anyway, because that was what you shouted.

Rickard dragged the bay's head round, putting the horse's shoulder toward the bowmen, and drew his sword right-handed. "Get behind the horse. Now."

The third man hadn't loosed. He was looking at Marro. Not at Rickard. At Marro, and at the splinted hand, as if checking something against a description.

The other two were nocking again. Slow about it. Men who'd been paid for the morning and didn't mean to hurry.

Blood ran warm into Marro's boot. The reeds hissed. Across the ford, the flash again, someone watching all of it.

## Turn 3

**Player:** I draw my dagger and go for the nearest one.

**GM:**

His right hand went to his belt for the dagger.

The splints knocked against leather. The sheath was empty. Of course it was empty. The dagger was at the bottom of the Rhoyne with Dagna, three nights down in the black water, and his hand had forgotten that, the way a hand forgets.

Half a heartbeat, fumbling at nothing. Long enough.

The nearest bowman saw it. Saw the boy reach and come up empty, and grinned, and stopped hurrying. Ten paces now. He drew to the ear. At that distance the arrow wouldn't graze anything.

Marro still had the cudgel. Left hand, wrong grip, the weight of it sitting badly in his palm. Ten paces of open gravel between him and the man, and the man already drawn.

Behind him, hooves. Rickard had put his heels to the bay, and the gelding came out from cover sideways and furious, spraying gravel. Rickard rode at the second bowman, not the first. Sword up in his good hand, reins in his teeth. The second man threw himself flat in the reeds.

That left the first one for Marro. And the third, the one who'd been studying his splinted hand, who had lowered his bow and was walking now, unhurried, along the bank toward him. A short blade had come out from under the grey cloak.

"The squire," the third man called. Volantene accent, easy, almost friendly. "Pallo sends his greetings. He wonders if you've found his silver yet."

The first man's bowstring creaked.

Ten paces. A drawn bow. A cudgel in the wrong hand, and a leg bleeding into his boot.
