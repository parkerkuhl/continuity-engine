**Chapter 2: The Long Bridge**

The new moon was four nights off, and Marro had four silver stags. He'd counted them twice before dawn, left-handed, because the right hand was still two splinted fingers and a dull ache. Counting them a third time wouldn't help, but he did it anyway.

The camp woke the way it always did. Cookfires, coughing, someone cursing a mule. The grain carts had come in overnight from the city side, Malaquo's seal pressed into the wax on every sack. Lysono Qhoran stood by the wagons with a ledger open against his forearm, writing down each sack as it came off. He didn't look up, but Marro had the feeling he'd been seen anyway.

Rickard was outside his tent, trying to buckle his own sword belt. His left arm hung stiff from the shoulder where the bolt had gone in, so he was doing it one-handed, badly.

"Don't," Rickard said, before Marro could reach for the buckle. Then, after a moment: "Fine. Do it."

Marro did it. Rickard smelled of old wine and the vinegar the surgeon used on the wound.

"Council at noon," Rickard said. "They want to know why the Triarch's men knew where we'd dig the second trench." He looked off toward the Black Wall, grey in the morning haze. "So do I."

Down the line Hesse was walking Ash in slow circles, the mare favoring her off foreline. Hesse's patch had slipped, and she shoved it back with her thumb without breaking stride. When she saw Marro she spat, which was how she said good morning.

"There's a man in grey at the picket," she said when he came near. "Asking for you by name. I told him you'd drowned." She watched him with her one eye. "He didn't believe me either."

Past the picket, where the road ran down toward the river, a grey cloak waited by the stump of a burned olive tree. He wasn't hiding, and that was worse.

Lissa's letter was folded inside Marro's shirt. He could feel it against his ribs. The Dragon's Den lay on the far side of the Long Bridge, and the bridge was closed to anyone without a stamped pass.

Then there was Rickard, waiting by the tent for his squire, with the council at noon.

## Turn 1

**Player:** Lysono Qhoran sends for me. I go to the paymaster's tent and wait to be spoken to.

**GM:**

The paymaster's tent was the only one in camp with a floor. Planks, laid over the mud, swept. A brazier burned in one corner, and the company's strongboxes sat along the back wall under a chained bar.

Lysono Qhoran was writing. He let Marro stand there while he finished the line, sanded it, blew the sand away, and only then looked up.

"Squire." His voice was soft enough that Marro had to stop breathing to hear it. "Your hand. How many fingers?"

"Two, ser."

"Two." He wrote something. Marro couldn't read it upside down from where he stood, and he suspected Lysono had angled the page for exactly that. "A dice-house, I'm told. Odd place for a squire who serves a captain so careful with coin."

He set the pen down square with the ledger's edge.

"Captain Snow keeps his own papers. Most captains give theirs to me. He does not." A small pause. "I don't hold it against him. A man likes his privacy. But the Triarch's men knew where the second trench would go, and very few people knew that. You sleep across his tent flap. You'd hear if he had visitors at night."

Outside, a sergeant was bawling about latrine duty. Lysono waited until it passed.

"I'm not asking you to accuse anyone. I'm asking what you've seen." He turned one page of the ledger, then another, and laid a finger on a line. "And I am aware a man in grey has been at our picket asking for you. Pallo's men wear grey. Debts are a weakness in a squire. A paymaster can make weaknesses go away, sometimes."

He folded his hands and waited, the brazier ticking in the quiet.

## Turn 2

**Player:** I ask him straight out whether anyone in the company is selling us to the Triarch.

**GM:**

Lysono didn't blink. He seemed to like the question, the way a man likes a coin that rings true when he drops it on the table.

"Yes," he said.

He let the word sit there a while.

"Twice now the Triarch has known our mind before we did. The second trench. Before that, the night we moved the rams up the east road and found the ditch already flooded." He touched the ledger again, not reading it, only resting his finger there. "Coincidence, once. Not twice. Someone with a seat at council, or close enough to one to hear it through canvas."

Close enough to hear it through canvas. Marro slept across a tent flap. He felt the words land on him, and he felt Lysono watching to see how they landed.

"I don't know who," the paymaster went on. "If I knew, he would already be in the river." The softness of his voice didn't change at all. "I have suspicions. I keep them where I keep the gold, under a bar, with a chain." His eyes went briefly to the strongboxes and back. "What I have, I'll share with a man who shares with me."

He picked up the pen, dipped it, and held it over a fresh line without writing.

"Council sits at noon. Your captain will speak. You'll stand behind him, as you always do. Afterward you could come back here and tell me who looked at whom, who asked too many questions about the trenches, who left early." The nib hovered. "Or you could go home to your tent and forget we spoke. I'll remember either way."

The brazier ticked.

## Turn 3

**Player:** I ask how he knows so much about what happens in Rickard's tent.

**GM:**

"I'm the paymaster," Lysono said. "Everyone in this company comes to me sooner or later. They want their wages, or an advance on their wages, or a horse." He set the pen down. "And everyone who comes to me talks."

That wasn't an answer, and he plainly knew it wasn't. Marro watched his face for whatever was underneath.

Wits, reading a schemer who doesn't want to be read: (3+5) +2 Wits = 10, against Lysono's 9. You beat him, but narrowly.

There. When Lysono said *a horse*, his eyes went to the left, toward the tent flap and past it, toward the horse lines, where you could hear a mare's uneven step on the hard ground, round and round. It was there and gone in a breath. He took it back by straightening the ledger, which didn't need straightening.

Marro's stomach went cold. Ash was lame. The company had no spare horses. And Hesse had been walking that mare in circles all morning, close enough to the paymaster's tent to wave.

It didn't prove anything. Half the camp needed a horse.

The cost came next. Lysono had seen Marro see it. He tilted his head a little, like a man noticing a crack in a cup he'd thought was whole.

"You're quicker than your captain gives you credit for," he said, very softly. "That's good. Quick men are useful, and they know better than to say every quick thing out loud." He opened a drawer and took out a small slate, wiped it with his sleeve, and laid it on the table between them. "The council bell will ring soon. Will you be coming back to me after?"

Outside, the mare's hoofbeats stopped.
